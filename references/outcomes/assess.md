# Assess defensibility, feasibility and build versus buy

Start with the value the user/customer gets and the conditions under which it
works. Decompose the important workflows into capabilities; investigate current
substitutes and the difficult gaps. Keep technical replicability distinct from
customer trust, operations and business advantage.

## Evidence and alternatives

Use actual product behavior, documentation, artifact/source evidence and relevant
external sources. Check current claims about alternatives against their official
docs/repos. Follow promising leads across independent reviews, partners,
interviews, datasets and publications, with dates and source incentives visible.
A founder story can suggest an advantage; it does not prove present exclusivity.

For material capabilities, compare named open-source components, services/SDKs,
integrated solutions, and custom work where relevant. Explain actual fit, limits,
data access, license/deployment constraints, effort and operational costs. Do not
fill every category with weak candidates just to complete a table. Verify the
critical options deeply and describe unverified leads honestly.

Evaluate potential advantages separately:

- Data: access, rights, quality, freshness, labels, feedback and acquisition cost.
- Technology: measured accuracy, difficult edge cases, latency, unique algorithms,
  infrastructure or hardware requirements, and reproducibility.
- Workflow: integration depth, accumulated configuration/history, human service,
  reliability and switching costs.
- Commercial position: distribution, brand, trust, certification, relationships
  and network effects where supported by evidence.

For each consequential moat hypothesis, state support, counterevidence, how a
credible alternative could overcome it, and what observation would change the
assessment. Present several mechanisms when warranted; the product need not have
one secret core. Commodity dependencies do not imply trivial product value.

## Deliverable and done

Produce a capability/alternative map, the hard gaps, and a reasoned recommendation
under the user's constraints. Describe the assets and work required to compete or
replace the target. Personalize using relevant user-provided context, never an
assumed employer, industry or resource base.

Use qualitative assessments backed by evidence. If the user explicitly requests
a numerical "wrapper percentage," define what is being measured, explain the
weights and uncertainty, and decline to quantify when the evidence cannot support
it. Do not equate a count of commodity components with a fraction of value.

Separate a useful prototype, a reliable private tool and a competitive service.
Finish with the most consequential unknown and the smallest useful experiment
that tests it. A build plan follows only when requested or selected as another
outcome; implementation follows an actual build instruction.

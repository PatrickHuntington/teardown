# Understand the stack, architecture or mechanism

Answer the particular "how" question. Determine whether the user wants a
technology inventory, architecture, algorithm, data flow, methodology, or
explanation of one feature. Use target-specific evidence rather than forcing
each question into a software component list.

## Investigation

Trace representative input -> relevant processing/state -> output, recording the
boundaries between modules, processes, services, people or document steps.
Explain responsibilities and dependencies. Include retries, review, persistence,
permissions or exceptions when they change how the mechanism works.

For a stack inventory, record component, role, evidence, version if known, and
confidence/unknowns. Distinguish shipped/client technology, backend evidence,
third-party services and platform dependencies. A framework marker cannot prove
the database, a provider URL cannot prove the exact model, and job advertisements
cannot establish the deployed version. Offer competing hypotheses when useful.

For an algorithm or methodology, expose assumptions, transformations, parameters,
units, required data and error cases. Work an example or perform a scoped test
when it materially strengthens the explanation and is possible. Label any
proposed equivalent as a design choice, separate from recovered behavior.

## Deliverable and done

Lead with the answer and its certainty. Show a compact architecture/mechanism
diagram when it clarifies relationships, a trace through the relevant layers,
and source locators for consequential claims. Tailor depth to the reader.
Explain what cannot be established and the observation that would resolve it.

Done means the requested mechanism/stack is supported to the available evidence
boundary and uncertainty is visible. Do not add a shopping list, moat verdict,
rebuild schedule or implementation unless another selected outcome needs it.
